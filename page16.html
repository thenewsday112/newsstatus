#376. This Is What the GOP’s Final Midterms Push Looks Like

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Republicans are spending the final week ahead of the midterm elections defending historically red Senate seats that have suddenly become competitive, according to federal filings and sources familiar with the matter.

A significant focus is now on four Senate races in ​K​ansas, ​South Carolina, ​Nebraska, and even ​Mississippi, all traditionally red seats, in what’s ​an ominous development for Republicans in their effort to at least keep control of the upper chamber.

Trump operatives tell Inner Loop that they do not expect ​Democrats to win those Senate seats and that they have ​mainly directed GOP outside groups to spend there to ​​help boost incumbents that only have a narrow lead.

But ​t​he operatives also concede that they are having to divert an increasing amount of resources ​to those states—states that president Donald Trump carried by double digits in 2024 and where Republicans never dreamed of having to spend tens of millions of dollars.

In total, in the penultimate week of the campaign, the Senate Republican–aligned Senate Leadership Fund spent $14 million across those four states, according to a WIRED review of federal filings. The biggest spend has been in Kansas, where SLF has abruptly dumped more than $5 million.

Trump operatives say Kansas has been a major headache since the start of October, when Trafalgar Group​, a GOP pollster, found Democratic candidate Adam Hamilton one point ahead of ​incumbent GOP senator Roger Marshall.

Marshall, a doctor, has had an unusually tough race following a slew of negative stories about how he pursued legal action against his patients with unpaid bills and how much time he spends in Florida. Some political operatives have also complained that he has not taken his race seriously, sources say.

​But the last-minute spending is also coming in places like South Carolina, where SLF spent another roughly $5 million to help incumbent Darline Graham, who has struggled to galvanize Trump's base despite having the president’s endorsement that propelled her to victory in the primary.

SLF also spent nearly $3 million in Nebraska to help Republican incumbent senator Pete Ricketts, who has been behind in some polls, and in Mississippi, the fund put just under $1 million toward Republican incumbent senator Cindy Hyde-Smith.

The final sprint has highlighted the fraught predicament for Republicans, who are having to patch a seemingly ever-growing number of vulnerabilities.

Trump's campaign schedule has also added several deep-red states that are flashing warning signs, in addition to the expected campaign stops in battleground states. Last week, Trump went to Kansas and South Carolina. This week, he has been in Mississippi and will return to Kansas and South Carolina, as well.

Still, several Trump operatives tell Inner Loop they have been encouraged by the fact that Democratic candidates lately have not expanded their leads in several key Senate races—a concern that has cropped up among some Senate Democratic leadership aides.

Chief among their concerns is Michigan, the best GOP pickup opportunity. Many of the most viable paths to Democrats taking the Senate majority pass through the state, but Democratic candidate Abdul El-Sayed has suffered lingering issues with key voting blocs.

Black voters turned out in droves to help Barack Obama carry the state in 2008 and 2012, and the conventional wisdom in Washington is that El-Sayed will need similar support in order to keep the seat blue, one of the Democratic aides tells Inner Loop.

But the trouble for El-Sayed, they add, has been the decades-long animosity between Arab and Black communities in Michigan, a divide that was further strained during the primary when supporters of his challenger, Haley Stevens, ran ads featuring Barack Obama praising her earlier work during the financial crisis.

In an effort to rebuild at least part of that coalition, Obama held a joint rally with El-Sayed in Detroit over the weekend, which aides are hoping could persuade some voters. Operatives aren’t sure if it’ll be enough.

#232. Israel's election deadlock is a sign of the country's deep divisions

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

"Democracy is the worst form of government except for all those other forms that have been tried." So said Winston Churchill in the House of Commons in 1947.

His aphorism came to mind as Israel's election results filtered through on Tuesday night - exit polls blending with ballots that were cast to dash or fulfil people's hopes and fears.

This election has, in some ways, been a celebration of democracy. Voters turning out in record numbers, Jews and Arabs alike. Many spent thousands to fly in from abroad to cast their ballot.

At polling stations across the country, we saw the passion of campaigners, as they used every minute to try to change that last mind and win that last vote.

President Isaac Herzog said the election was a "badge of honour" for Israeli society. "It is remarkable to see how deep the desire for democracy is in Israel," he exclaimed.

Yet this election left the most established democracy in the Middle East without a clear path to a new government. It produced an inconclusive result where neither opposing bloc will have a majority in parliament.

Some might say this was business as usual.

Israel has never had a one-party government. Its fragmented, proportional representation system is almost designed to govern by coalition.

Yet many commentators warned this election had revealed the fundamental and perhaps growing divisions that scar this nation. This was not a plebiscite in which an electorate united around a single cause.

Israel's election result is messy - here's why

Some of these fissures are not new. There were divisions over judicial reforms that led to mass demonstrations in the early years of this government.

There were also divisions over how to respond to the deadly 7 October 2023 attacks - whether to save hostages or destroy Hamas. The casualties suffered by Israeli reservists in the fighting in Gaza that followed deepened tensions over the exemption of ultra-Orthodox young men from military service.

But has this election put in even sharper contrast the deep fault-lines that lie within Israeli society?

There are the tensions between the religious and the secular, divides not just of faith but of society.

Should Israel's government subsidise religious study? Should some parents sacrifice their sons and daughters to defend Israel when others do not? The ultra-Orthodox now make up almost 15% of the population. Their world-view is poles apart from that of denizens of high-tech, modern neighbourhoods of Tel Aviv.

There are divisions over fundamental politics.

Benjamin Netanyahu has called for "a strong right-wing national government"

Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud party suffered significant losses, but is still on track to be the largest in the Knesset. All this for a prime minister accused of corruption, which he denies; a leader also accused of failing to protect Israel before 7 October, something he will not allow to be investigated.

Some 10% of voters backed the Jewish supremacist far-right: politicians like Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich, who even as results were being announced was promising to establish Jewish settlements in Gaza.

The Arab parties, which represent about a fifth of Israeli citizens, made huge gains, winning some 15% of the vote, potentially giving them seats numbering in the high teens.

And then there was the opposition bloc, a mix of centre, left and right led by Gadi Eisenkot who fell short of winning the change they wanted - change that many believed was existential for the future of this country.

Gadi Eisenkot declared that the public had "spoken in favour of change"

How these vastly diverging views coalesce into some form of government is now the unenviable task facing Israel's body politic - the choices and compromises they may have to make could involve fundamental decisions about the country's future.

Would Netanyahu be ready to abandon his religious allies and form a coalition with right-wingers from the opposition bloc? Would they be ready to swallow their hostility towards the prime minister and sit in his government?

Is the opposition bloc ready to abandon much of its campaign promises and co-operate with Arab parties to force Netanyahu out of office? Are the Arab parties ready to risk joining a government that could fall within months?

Yair Golan, the left-wing Democrat leader, urged the opposition bloc to form a partnership with Arab parties. "We must no longer accept Netanyahu's racist division between Jewish votes and Arab votes," he said.

But are the people of Israel ready for such a reality?

Amit Segal, an Israeli commentator sympathetic towards Netanyahu, said the high turnout among Arab Israeli voters "reminded Jews on both sides that, after years of division, they may have to learn to sit together after all".

The risk is that this election, instead of putting Israel on a new path, has instead raised further doubts about what it is that unites its citizens. What are the common factors that bind this complex, varied collection of peoples into one polity?

Autocratic governments, which are growing in number, have long criticised democracies for producing weak and temporary governments, often fragile coalitions that struggle to hold together, let alone deliver what the people wanted.

Israel may now struggle for months to form a government. It might even fail and have to hold another election.

President Herzog acknowledged this risk head on as he prepared to open coalition negotiations: "We must do everything we can, certainly after yesterday, to avoid another round of elections."

For years, Israel has focused on defending itself against its external enemies. But must it also now be ready to defend itself from perils within, if the divisions writ large by this election become unmanageable?

In another speech, Churchill once said democracy was not just "insurance against dangers from abroad". It was also, he added, "insurance against dangers scarcely less grave and much more near and constant which threaten us here at home".

#179. YouTube missed expectations on ad revenue as growth slowed down

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

YouTube's ad revenue growth slowed down, missing analysts' estimates in the third quarter.

Its parent company, Alphabet, reported $11.2 billion in third-quarter YouTube ads revenue, a 9% increase from a year ago. That falls below the $11.43 billion analyst consensus estimate. Its ads revenue growth also slowed from the previous quarter, when it pulled in about $11.1 billion, a 13% increase from the previous year.

Beyond YouTube, Alphabet missed expectations in other areas of its business, including on overall revenue. In a bright spot, it beat revenue estimates for its cloud business.

The company's shares were down over 10% in after-hours trading.

YouTube has also been the envy of rivals in key areas like TV viewership. Its share of US television viewership was about twice that of Netflix in August, according to recent Nielsen estimates. Over a third of YouTube's third-quarter ad revenue came from the living room, Google's chief business officer Philipp Schindler said on Wednesday.

Netflix, meanwhile, walked back its full-year ad revenue expectations during last week's earnings. The streamer lowered its ad revenue forecast from $3 billion to $2.7 billion after experiencing delays around the rollout of its ad products. It laid off about 1,000 workers earlier this month.

This week, Instagram unveiled an overhaul of long-form video features seemingly designed to counter YouTube and bolster its presence on TV screens.

"Everyone in streaming is chasing YouTube, and even Instagram is going to pivot their strategy based on the success that YouTube's had," said Ross Benes, a senior analyst at EMARKETER, Business Insider's sister company.

#302. DoorDash's deal to buy Instacart coming as early as Thursday morning, sources tell CNBC

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

DoorDash's bid to buy Instacart could be announced as soon as Thursday morning, sources familiar with the matter told CNBC.

Speculation of a deal has swirled since last week, when The Information reported Instacart was exploring a sale and had held talks with companies including Uber and DoorDash. On Monday, several outlets reported DoorDash was in advanced discussions to acquire Instacart and that the deal could value Instacart at about $17 billion.

Instacart's stock popped about 4% in extended trading on Wednesday, while DoorDash shares fell less than 1%.

Instacart declined to comment. Representatives from DoorDash didn't immediately respond to CNBC's request for comment.

Acquiring Instacart would give DoorDash a bigger presence in online grocery delivery. DoorDash got its start in food delivery, and since then it has added more retailers to its marketplace, such as Sephora, Macy's, Staples and Anthropologie. In recent months, it's brought on a growing stable of regional grocers, after announcing a partnership with Kroger last year.

Instacart, which went public in 2023, was one of the biggest beneficiaries of a pandemic-driven surge in grocery delivery services, but it has struggled to maintain that momentum. In December Instacart agreed to pay $60 million to settle allegations by the Federal Trade Commission that it misled users with false advertising and deployed "unlawful subscription enrollment" practices.

The service has also faced increasingly stiff competition from companies like Amazon and Walmart. After slowing its brick-and-mortar grocery effort, Amazon has doubled down on its online grocery business and leveraged its vast fulfillment footprint to speed up deliveries of household essentials, pantry staples and perishables.

Both Amazon and Walmart have pushed further into same- and next-day delivery, which poses a threat to Instacart's on-demand delivery model.

Analysts have noted that a potential combination of Instacart and DoorDash could attract antitrust scrutiny given the companies' market overlap.

Third-party delivery platforms "remain a minority of total grocery consumption, but slicing and dicing the online market more narrowly or from a perspective of the grocers as enterprise customers can make approvals more challenging," Wedbush analysts wrote in a Monday research note.

A fresh look at cellphone data and text messages led to the arrest of a Florida man accused of murdering his ex-fiancée within hours of taping an episode for “The People’s Court” in 2011, court documents unsealed Tuesday revealed.

Dale Smith Jr., a 55-year-old Orlando resident, was taken into custody Oct. 14 and charged with second-degree murder in the death of Michelle Parker, whose body has never been found, police said.

Shares of the company fell slightly in extended trading.

Here's how the company performed for the period ended Sept. 30 compared with what Wall Street was expecting, based on a survey of analysts by LSEG:

The company reported net income for the quarter of $290 million, or $1.21 per share, compared with $650 million, or $2.73 per share, during the comparable period a year prior. To appeal to those folks, Apple didn't just integrate a touch feature and OLED display and call it a day. For the new models, Apple used tandem OLED technology (first employed on the M4 iPad Pro) that stacks multiple organic layers into a single display panel. There are several key benefits to tandem OLED tech, namely high peak HDR brightness, a lower burn-in risk and a longer panel lifespan. Its advanced display engine also controls each pixel's color and luminance "at the sub-millisecond level" to compensate for content, temperature and environment. That tech also addresses a key problem with OLED (organic pixel degradation over time), by adapting each pixel's age and usage.

The new laptops use Apple's 120Hz ProMotion display for reasonably high refresh rates when gaming or creating content. And if you work outside at times or in a bright environment, you can get the new models with Apple's nano-texture option that's "now precisely etched into the cover glass," Apple says, to further reduce glare.

The new MacBook Pros with PROLED XDR touch displays probably aren't worth the higher $400 base price for most people. With all the tech it's crammed into the displays, though, the models will be awfully tempting for users who make their money by creating content.

#386. Most Servers on the Planet Are Vulnerable to This Firmware Code-Execution Flaw

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Defenders across the world face the burden of responding to yet another botched code-execution vulnerability, this time in boot-time firmware used by most of the world’s server vendors and data centers.

The vulnerability, with a severity rating of 9.5 out of 10, resides in the TianoCore EDK II, an open source codebase that hardware makers and cloud providers use to create the UEFI firmware that runs during the boot-up sequence. Last year, a representative of the UEFI Forum called EDK2 the “preeminent solution / implementation of the UEFI specifications.” Virtually all UEFI suppliers (sometimes still called IBVs or independent BIOS vendors), including AMI, Insyde, and Phoenix, use the reference code extensively. Project Mu, Microsoft’s hardened EDK2 fork, used the code containing the vulnerable component as well.

The vulnerability, tracked as CVE-2026-97631, is a memory-corruption flaw in an ICMP component that attackers can exploit to execute malicious code that runs before the operating system and firmware can implement defenses like Secure Boot, which establishes a chain of trust for all code, including the Trusted Platform Module (TPM), loaded during boot. While final payloads delivered by CVE-2026-97631 have no persistence—meaning they run only once—they can give attackers a wide opening to install other payloads that also run each time early in the boot process.

The parties that discovered CVE-2026-97631—Alex Matrosov of Anthropic and Dan Holmes of Google—had arranged for it to stay under wraps until a coordinated disclosure that was scheduled for January. AMI, a maker of motherboards and other hardware, published this pull request last month that detailed the vulnerability and included a code fix. Once the post came to the attention of the discovering parties, TianoCore Project members published a public advisory last week.

With publicly available patch code, it will be trivial for advanced hackers to learn how the vulnerability is triggered and to build working exploits. Further compounding the urgency, patching UEFI code vulnerabilities in millions of end devices sold and used by thousands of hardware makers and cloud providers is cumbersome and time-consuming. Word of the vulnerability comes six days after another botched disclosure, this one involving a critical code-execution vulnerability in the PHP programming language.

“This vulnerability in the ICMP parser of the EDK2 network stack is interesting in terms of how it affects the chain of trust and the broader ecosystem,” said HD Moore, creator of Metasploit and chief research officer at security firm Dragos. “An attacker intent on compromising a nearby endpoint or server could use this bug to hijack program flow at a point where the boot process still has access to sensitive components, like the endpoint’s TPM. This could be used to break disk encryption for cases where an attacker has short-term network access (and it would be even easier in a theft scenario).”

CVE-2026-97631 is a 16-year-old buffer overflow in the PXE-Base Code driver (NetworkPkg/UefiPxeBcDxe), which is responsible for allowing servers, storage equipment, and other devices to boot using code downloaded from a local network. During this process—known as the pre-execution environment boot—the driver uses a network listener to monitor for ICMP errors. Because the driver doesn’t enforce a memory copy limit of 504 bytes, overwriting adjacent data, a malicious insider or a hacker who has gained low-level access to a local network can send an error message that appends code past this limit. The IUEFI module running on the CPU then becomes corrupted.

During network boot, the PXE Base Code driver (NetworkPkg/UefiPxeBcDxe) keeps a receive request open for ICMP error messages and copies each received message into the fixed 504-byte field Mode.IcmpError of its private context structure. The copy loop’s bounds handling is incorrect in both the IPv4 and IPv6 handlers (PxeBcIcmpErrorDpcHandle and PxeBcIcmp6ErrorDpcHandle): for a message longer than 504 bytes it still copies bytes into the field, and it advances the destination pointer by the cumulative rather than the per-fragment length, so an oversized message writes attacker-controlled bytes past the field. Nothing earlier on the receive path restricts the message length.

The malicious error can be delivered through a simple unicast sent from any network distance to a target device’s IP address, as long as it’s sent during the PXE boot sequence. An attacker can deliver a denial-of-service by causing the boot sequence to hang. When the attacker knows what the target firmware build is, it can plant a “counterfeit event” that executes the malicious code on the CPU in its UEFI firmware environment. The code runs in ring 0, a CPU’s most privileged execution level. Because exploits bypass attestation processes designed to cryptographically prove to remote servers that the hardware is secure, they present a key opportunity for elevating limited privileges.

CVE-2026-97631 joins a long list of previously discovered vulnerabilities in UEFI or code that works closely with it. The most severe of them, LogoFail, stemmed from logo images included in the UEFI of just about every hardware maker. Rendering bugs allowed attackers to subvert the chain of trust UEFI enforces and go on to install malicious components before the OS loaded or Secure Boot ran. PixieFail was a vulnerability similar to CVE-2026-97631. It, too, was the result of faulty PXE boot flaws in EDK2. Following discovery of the vulnerability, researchers embarked on multiple fuzzing projects designed to surface other critical flaws in the open source code library. It appears those efforts failed to find this latest flaw.

Like CVE-2026-97631, exploitation or use of these payloads is appealing because it allows attackers to infect machines early in the boot process, before TPM and other defenses load, so they can inflict maximum damage. Given the ubiquity of CVE-2026-97631, Moore said it’s likely a “forever” bug that will live on in end-of-support systems.

There are important caveats and limitations to exploiting this latest vulnerability. As noted, some level of control over a local network is a prerequisite. That likely limits exploitation to insiders or attackers, such as nation-states or ransomware operators, who manage to gain low-level access inside a network and are looking for a way to elevate system rights. There’s no evidence yet that CVE-2026-97631 provides an initial entry point into a network. There are other hurdles to clear.

“For exploiting the vulnerability itself, an attacker needs to guess specific addresses within the target system, so it’s not something that you can throw a single exploit at any target and get RCE across a diverse range of devices, but it needs to be tuned for each target model/version/configuration since the addresses will change based on those,” Jesse Michael, a principal researcher at firmware security firm Eclypsium, told Ars.

“Unlike some of the previous, high-profile vulnerabilities like BootHole, Black Lotus, and Baton Drop, this vulnerability can be fixed with a basic code fix, which will require deploying a BIOS/UEFI update, but they don’t need to deal with revocations, pk/kek/db/dbx key management updates, or making sure to update OS and provisioning media like you do with Secure Boot bypasses in the bootloader itself.”

CVE-2026-97631 doesn’t affect home and small office users, or any other users who boot their devices solely from disk drives. Instead, it applies only to organizations with machines that boot from local networks, or conceivably from Internet-connected resources. Even then, the targeted firmware must rely on EDK2, as opposed to a different reference implementation, and use the PXE functionality (which is turned on by default).

In an email, Intel said that none of its “commercial products currently under security support” are known to be affected. “Intel does not include the affected EDK2 functionality in its reference code, though it may be integrated by an OEM board vendor or system manufacturer,” the company added. It advised customers to check with their system manufacturer for specific guidance. Publicly available Intel reference code, however, makes reference to EDK2. Further, TianoCore has said that each Intel-supplied UEFI development kit “is a fully validated implementation … for a specific revision of the EDK II source.” Many other Intel documents reference EDK2 as well.

Arm, meanwhile, responded to a list of questions with: “CVE-2026-97631 is not directly applicable to Arm’s product portfolio.” Like Intel, firmware the company makes available also borrows heavily from EDK2. TianoCore has also documented Arm’s reliance on EDK2.

While it’s possible some platforms are free of EDK2 code, it’s prudent for users of all servers to investigate if CVE-2026-97631 affects them.

Questions sent to AMD, Phoenix Technologies, AMI, Microsoft, and Amazon—likely some of the other biggest organizations affected—went unanswered when this post went live. Without concrete answers to questions about the use of EDK2 by organizations like these, it’s impossible to say what or how devices are affected and what actions, if any, these organizations, or their users, should take. Representatives from the TianoCore Project also didn’t respond.

Some readers are likely to be unhappy at the lack of detail. Sometimes—particularly in cases involving botched disclosures that break embargoes months before they’re scheduled to lift—there’s no escaping the lack of actionable information in initial reporting.

Those affected should check with the maker of the firmware being used to boot their systems and ask for guidance. Beyond that, they should consider turning off network boot capabilities at least temporarily if possible. Firmware configurations should always enable security features such as TPM and Secure Boot whenever possible, although those defenses don’t protect against this particular threat. It’s also a good idea to monitor logs for signs of exploitation attempts.
