#491. Rachel Zoe Admits She ‘Didn’t Even Think About’ Signing Prenup With Ex

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Rachel Zoe and her estranged husband, Rodger Berman, admittedly had no reason to sign a prenuptial agreement before they got married.

“If I could rewind, like, I would do that so differently,” Zoe, 55, said on the Tuesday, October 28, episode of the “Aspire” podcast. “I think that in marriage, what I’m finding now, friends of mine, they’re like, ‘I have my money, he has his money, and then there’s ours.’ That is so beautiful and brilliant because you don’t know what’s going to happen. No one knows what’s going to happen.”

Zoe and Berman, 57, announced in 2024 that they separated after two decades of marriage. The now-exes, who share two sons, have nearly reached a divorce settlement.

“The reason that I didn’t have a prenup is because we met when I was 19 … [and] we were kids,” the stylist explained of not signing a premarital agreement. “He was waiting tables, I was in college.”

Once Zoe started making a living as a fashion stylist and a Bravo star, she admittedly “didn’t even think about” adding a postnuptial clause to protect her assets.

“We were dying next to each other. We were what everybody called ‘The Notebook couple.’ There was not a world that we were not, like, dying holding hands, you know?” Zoe stressed. “I mean, there is a world, and that world is now.”

As Zoe and Berman navigate their divorce, they have remained focused on coparenting their children.

“I would say that being a single mom for me has been easier than not … because every decision I make is mine and ours, me and the boys,” the Real Housewives of Beverly Hills star noted. “There’s a tremendous responsibility that women have for their husbands. I can’t speak for every woman, but I can tell you that so much of my emotional spend was on my ex-husband.”

She continued, explaining, “It means that when you love someone or when you look after someone, when you care about someone, when you think about someone, when you everything someone who is your other half, …if you remove that part, there is a huge chunk that you don’t have to be conscious of anymore.”

According to Zoe, “considering someone else’s opinion and thoughts” on all of her decisions had taken a toll on her mental headspace.

“I feel this immense weight is off of me, so I would absolutely say, with all confidence, that being a single mom for me has been 10x easier than being a married one,” Zoe said. “I do [feel free]. I think that, when it comes to finances, there is a trap that women can get in because they don’t know, they don’t want to know [or because] he was the finance person, and there’s a trust in that.”

#312. Chipotle shares rise 7% after CEO says same-store sales growth will continue

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Chipotle Mexican Grill on Wednesday reported mixed quarterly results and said its restaurant-level operating margin shrank.

But the burrito chain saw traffic and same-store sales grow during the quarter as low-income consumers spent more, bucking industry trends, and CEO Scott Boatwright projected a strong finish to the year.

Shares of the company rose 7% in extended trading.

"Broader consumer and industry headwinds may continue, but initiatives across our recipe for growth strategy are building and giving us more tools to win in any environment," Boatwright said on the company's earnings conference call. "We are still in the early stages of this work, and it's gaining traction."

Like the broader restaurant industry, Chipotle saw trends soften in early October, although executives said its traffic has returned to growth since then. Boatwright said the company has "high confidence" in its performance for the rest of the fourth quarter.

"We feel very confident that November and December set up very well for us," Boatwright said.

Chief Financial Officer Adam Rymer said the company expects fourth-quarter same-store sales growth of about 2%.

Here's what the company reported for the period ended Sept. 30 compared with what Wall Street was expecting, based on a survey of analysts by LSEG:

Chipotle reported fiscal third-quarter net income of $339.9 million, or 27 cents per share, down from $382.1 million, or 29 cents per share, a year earlier. The company's restaurant-level operating margin fell from 24.5% to 23.3%.

Excluding restaurant asset impairment charges and stock-based compensation, the company earned 27 cents per share.

Net sales rose 9.6% to $3.29 billion, fueled by the chain's new restaurants. During the quarter, Chipotle opened 84 company-owned restaurants and five international locations run by licensees.

In the second half of July, Chipotle said it started to see sales pull back as diners grew concerned about consuming raw lettuce due to cyclospora, although its own supply was never tied to an outbreak. Then in August, the chain disclosed that it had temporarily pulled jalapenos from Minnesota restaurants after learning its supply was potentially linked to a salmonella outbreak.

Still, the chain was able to report same-store sales growth for its third straight quarter, shaking off last year's traffic woes.

Sales at stores open at least 13 months increased 2.1%, lifted by a 1.3% bump in average check.

For the first time this year, low-income consumers during the quarter outperformed the high-income cohort at its restaurants, Boatwright said. And discounts aren't the reason — low-income consumers spent the most in September, when promotions were at their low point during the quarter, he told analysts.

For the full year, Chipotle reiterated its previous forecast that its same-store sales will increase by a low-single-digit percentage.

Looking ahead to 2027, the company is projecting that it will open between 340 and 360 new company-owned locations in the U.S. and Canada and around 15 to 25 international restaurants.

#236. Democratic women could make history in midterm governor and House races

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

They're chasing a record number of governorships and 100 House seats.

Four years ago, eight Democratic women won governorships in a single election, a milestone that reshaped the map.

This November, Democrats aim to elect even more -- including in red states -- and to push the number of Democratic women in the U.S. House past 100. Candidates and organizations on the left told ABC News both milestones are plausible, but they rest on winning the most competitive races.

Twenty-six women are major-party nominees overall, one more than the 2022 record of 25. There are 19 Democratic women gubernatorial nominees, a record that's up from 16 in 2022. Republicans have seven women nominees in governor's races, down from nine in 2022.

Kelly Dittmar of Rutgers’ Center for American Women and Politics said Democrats have “a more robust support infrastructure” than Republicans specifically targeting women in politics, including trainings, recruitment programs, PACs and funding, which she says strengthens the pipeline to elected office.

Sarah Chamberlain, a GOP strategist and CEO of the Republican Main Street Partnership, argues that “finding Republican women who want to run” is the challenge rather the party's support infrastructure.

“The numbers are not as high as the Democratic women, and they've really never been, but absolutely, there is an entire Republican Party … open to welcoming women, and it's just that the candidates this year tended to be more men,” Chamberlain said.

In Ohio, Republican nominee Vivek Ramaswamy has faced bipartisan backlash for his attack ad against Democratic nominee Amy Acton. The ad features Acton's estranged mother, whom Acton says stood by the man criminally charged with sexually abusing her as a child before his death in 2004.

Acton would be Ohio's first woman governor and has railed against Ramaswamy for doubling down on the ad in recent speeches, including at her rally alongside former President Barack Obama.

She told ABC News: "I'm a scrappy kid from Youngstown. I've seen a thing or two, and I'm up for this fight. But kindness shouldn't be seen as weakness."

Michigan Democrat Jocelyn Benson said in an interview that her miscarriage has shaped her healthcare message when speaking to voters. She noted that federal funding cuts have closed three Planned Parenthood clinics in the state, including the only one in the Upper Peninsula.

"Behind every clinic closure is a woman who has to drive two extra hours for care or go without it," Benson said.

New Jersey Gov. Mikie Sherrill, the state's first Democratic woman governor, said party strategists drew the wrong lesson after 2024. Too many insiders concluded that Democrats needed to be crass and create a "male bro culture" to look tough, she said, but that approach has been unsuccessful while "steely-eyed women" have been successful. Voters, she says, value authenticity.

Several nominees could break barriers. Gina Hinojosa would be the first Latina governor of Texas. In New Mexico, former Interior Secretary Deb Haaland would be the first Native American woman to serve as a governor. Keisha Lance Bottoms would be Georgia's first woman governor and the first Black woman to govern any state.

"We didn't believe as a country that the nation would elect a Black president ... and it happened. So it's possible, it just hasn't happened yet. And for me, it's much more personal ... We can trace our family to a plantation in Crawfordville, Georgia," Bottoms told ABC's Linsey Davis.

Massachusetts Gov. Maura Healey chairs the Democratic Governors Association's Women Governors Fund. Since 2018, the DGA has invested over $120 million to elect and re-elect the current class of Democratic women governors.

The DGA has put $1 million into Texas, but the group and its chair, Kentucky Gov. Andy Beshear, have faced pressure from some prominent Latino Democrats to invest $10 million in Hinojosa's race.

Appearing with Bottoms at a rally in Georgia on Monday, Beshear announced the DGA was investing $5 million more into her race, which followed an announcement earlier this month of a $10 million investment in Bottoms' race against billionaire businessman Rick Jackson.

Healey told ABC News, "Gina is a great candidate, and I believe she can win. And it is to her credit that she has made this a winnable race."

Ninety-five Democratic women currently serve as voting members in the House, and 198 Democratic women are on the general election ballot. Elect Democratic Women, a political action committee that supports Democratic women candidates, hope to elect at least 100 women to the House -- which would be the most ever.

As for Republican women, there are 30 voting members currently serving in the House, down from 34 in the 118th Congress. There are 73 GOP women on the general election ballot. Dittmar maintains that a new record for Republican women is unlikely, and a drop from current levels is possible given the political headwinds facing Republicans this year.

Chamberlain acknowledged Republicans' uphill climb, saying, “If the polling is right, some [Republican] women and a whole lot of men are going to lose their seats."

Rep. Lois Frankel, who chairs Elect Democratic Women, said it supports women candidates both emotionally and financially. She argued that women's presence in government matters.

"Women were excluded from medical research until Pat Schroeder and Nancy Pelosi got on Appropriations [Committee] and fought for them," Frankel said.

Denise Powell, the Democratic candidate in Nebraska's 2nd District, would be the first woman to hold the seat. She said the group's support carried her through a six-way primary.

Powell, whose children are 16 and 12, talks often on the trail about motherhood. She argues too few mothers of young kids serve in Congress, "and I think that explains why there are so many policies that do nothing to support working parents."

A point-and-shoot digital camera is typically small and compact, with a built-in lens and features like automatic photo and video modes, according to our experts. This is different from DSLR and mirrorless cameras, which can range in size and weight and have larger, removable lenses.

I focus on small and compact point-and-shoot cameras in this guide, but there are other kinds on the market too, including “super zoom” models with longer, bulkier lenses.

To pick the best point-and-shoot cameras on the market, I tried models from brands like Canon, Sony, Fujifilm, Kodak and Panasonic throughout the year. Then, to learn more about cameras and current trends, I spoke with Steinberg (note: B&H Photo & Video is one of the biggest camera stores in the U.S) and chose cameras based on the following criteria:

Cameras, even within a sub-category like point-and-shoot, can vary in shape, size and function. The Om System Tough TG-7 and Panasonic Lumix ZS99 may have a similar price tag, but they look, feel and function very differently. Below are a few things our experts recommend keeping in mind as you shop for your next camera.

A camera that’s great for taking family portraits may not also be great at photographing far-off wildlife. Before you purchase any camera, it’s important to understand what you are looking to photograph or film.

If you want to shoot photos of wildlife, look for a camera with a long zoom range — professional wildlife lenses typically have a minimum zoom length of at least 200mm, according to B&H Photo & Video. If you plan on mostly shooting video, consider choosing a camera with a flip-up screen that makes it easier to film yourself, and one that can shoot video in a 4K resolution. If you love the detail of a sunset or texture of flower petals, look for a camera with a larger image sensor, it’s going to capture those details with more clarity, in my experience.

I’ve tested enough cameras to know that sometimes it’s all about feel. Some cameras feel great in my hands, some don’t. Before purchasing your next camera, consider shopping in-person so you can physically hold and test your options, says Steinberg.

All digital cameras have an image sensor inside. When you take a photo, light goes through the lens and hits the digital image sensor inside, which creates the digital image. A bigger image sensor typically means a more detailed photo (especially in a darker environment) and a wider range of highlights and shadows (dynamic range).

But a larger image sensor usually means a bigger and more expensive camera. See the most common image sensor types below, listed from largest to smallest:

Full-frame (36x22mm). Includes most mirrorless cameras from Canon, Sony and Nikon, especially professional models.

APS-C (24x16mm). Includes the Fujifilm X100 series, Ricoh GR series and many mirrorless cameras from Fujifilm, Sony, Nikon and Canon.

Micro four thirds (17x13mm). Includes the Leica D-Lux 8 and many mirrorless cameras from OM System and Panasonic.

1-inch sensor (12.8x9.6mm). Includes the Sony RX series and Canon PowerShot series.

Less than 1- inch sensor. Includes smartphones, Kodak Pixpro series, Panasonic ZS series, OM System Tough series and more.

This is different from resolution or megapixels — cameras can have the same sized image sensor but a different amount of megapixel.

There are pros and cons to point-and-shoot cameras and phone cameras.

New, high-end phones may take “better” photos and videos than some point-and-shoot cameras, according to Steinberg.

However, if you are looking to use your photos outside of social media, look for a camera that can shoot raw image files, says photo editor Birnbaum. Raw files are the purest form of a digital image, and are much more detailed and well-suited to photo editing and printing. Most phone cameras cannot shoot raw image files, while most dedicated cameras can.

Point-and-shoot cameras are typically more ergonomic than phone cameras — they are easier to hold and easier to control, says Steinberg. That’s likely because phones were never designed with photography as their sole purpose.

Outside of models like the Om System Tough TG-7, most cameras aren’t totally weatherproof. By contrast, nearly all smartphones are, with high IP ratings to match.

Ultimately, our experts agree that the best camera is the one you have with you. “If you don’t have your camera ready, you miss your shot,” says Steinberg.

She continued, “It’s because I’ve been laughing and smiling and feeling alive again. That’s why we get those wrinkles. If I was to be a corpsey, like I want my daughter to see me … I want her to see all these lines because it means I’m living and laughing, you know, I just have a very deep appreciation for life now when I’ve been so sick.”

#144. Fed plans to eliminate some rate-setting meetings, chairman says

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The Federal Reserve plans to eliminate some meetings devoted to setting interest rates in the next couple of years, Chairman Kevin Warsh said Wednesday.

Starting next fall, the central bank will dedicate one of its eight annual meetings to considering broad-based questions about the economy, rather than to voting on interest rates, Warsh said. That number will increase to two in 2028.

The chairman made the announcement following the release of the central bank’s latest policy announcement, which left rates unchanged.

He said the rationale for doing so was to devote more time to thinking about longer-term issues by shifting attention away from the immediate interest rate environment.

“Getting policy right involves assessing near-term developments at timely, regular and information-rich intervals,” he said. “But it’s equally important we reserve time and space as an intellectually diverse group for strategic deliberations, for thinking anew about big questions, looking out over longer time horizons.”

Warsh’s announcement marks a major shake-up for the Federal Reserve’s long-standing policy calendar, which is closely watched by investors around the world. The Federal Open Market Committee has held eight scheduled meetings a year since 1981.

Warsh began seriously discussing a reduction in rate-setting meetings in July, according to the FOMC’s meeting minutes, arguing that a longer lag between rate-setting meetings would allow the committee to accumulate more data about the economy.

Reducing the number of rate-setting meetings has long been debated over concerns about shortsightedness — but the switch was never enacted over fears that the Fed would become less nimble in reacting to sudden changes in the economy.

The change would make the Fed unique among major central banks, including the Bank of England and Bank of Japan.

It marks another major change enacted by Warsh, who took the reins of the Fed in May after being nominated by President Donald Trump.
