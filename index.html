Football rarely follows the script to the final curtain. At Soldier Field, Orlando City looked to have written a first half of ruthless efficiency, only for Chicago Fire to tear up the pages after the break. It was a night of contrasts, with the clinical elegance of the visitors eventually met by the stubborn persistence of the home side.

Griezmann pulls the strings as Orlando strike early

Orlando needed just six minutes to breach the Chicago defence. Iván Angulo found the net after being set up by Antoine Griezmann, who was involved from the start. Chicago held the bulk of possession and the better of the chances, but they looked vulnerable every time Orlando broke forward.

Griezmann turned from provider to finisher in the 35th minute. Receiving the ball outside the box, he curled a shot past Chris Brady for a 2-0 lead. It was set up by Vinícius Nogueira, and Griezmann's assist for Angulo earlier had been his 12th straight MLS match with a goal contribution, tying the club record set by Martín Ojeda in 2025.

Lewandowski denied as Chicago search for a way back

Chicago's most dangerous player in the first half was Robert Lewandowski. He started up front and had two clear chances in the opening 20 minutes, a header that came to nothing and a spectacular scissor kick that rattled the post. Orlando did not drop deep after the equaliser, but Chicago kept the pressure on late in the game. Orlando's David Brekalo was booked in the 76th minute, and neither side could find a winner.

The point leaves Chicago fourth in the Eastern Conference with two games left. Orlando extended their unbeaten run to five matches and are close to securing a playoff place. At times, the lack of pass rush can partly explain Washington's struggles in the secondary. After a week off, the Commanders host the Philadelphia Eagles Sunday night for a rematch of Week 1.

Head coach Dan Quinn, known for his ability to pressure opposing passers as a former defensive coordinator, said Washington can improve defensively, particularly the pass rush, coming out of the bye.

"I'm not pleased with where we're at, but I also know there's plenty to go," Quinn said on Wednesday. "I'd love to see us, whether it's blitzing, rush coverage together, really in sync with that. Philadelphia's offensive line will look much different than it did in Week 1. Hall of Fame right tackle Lane Johnson retired. Cam Jurgens was traded. There have been injuries. The Eagles could give the Commanders' pass rush a chance to get right on Sunday night.
