#74. Manhunt grows for escaped murderer who may have left area where truck was ditched

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Jacob Morley, a convicted killer on the loose since Sunday, has no known connection to the small Illinois town where the truck he is accused of stealing was found, the police chief said Wednesday.

“What we have seen kind of leads us in the direction that he’s not still here. But that doesn’t mean that we’ve stopped looking,” Marion Police Chief David Fitts said.

Morley, 28, convicted of killing a co-worker in 2022, was serving a life sentence when he escaped from Northwest Correctional Complex in Tiptonville, Tennessee, sometime before 11:30 p.m. Sunday, officials there said.

Morley had stolen a work truck, and early Tuesday that truck was found behind a business in Marion, a town of around 16,000 that is around 100 miles north of Tiptonville, authorities in Marion and Tennessee said.

There have been no reports in Marion of any other stolen vehicles, and there was no evidence around the truck that was found, Fitts said.

There have also been no confirmed sightings, he said.

“There’s really nowhere for him to go here where he wouldn’t be recognized. His face is all over the news,” Fitts said.

Morley has no known connection to Marion or southern Illinois, he said.

“Have no idea why he would come here, other than I-57 is a major interstate and it runs right through the middle of our town,” Fitts said. “And I don’t know, maybe he was low on gas and just pulled off. I don’t know.”

Morley was convicted of first-degree murder, abuse of a corpse, tampering with evidence and aggravated burglary after a trial in February 2025. He also pleaded guilty to theft of property.

He was accused of shooting Cody Birchfield in the back of the head in 2022, according to court documents. Birchfield’s body was found in a large metal box under Morley’s residence, the documents say.

Birchfield’s sister, Carrie Norman, said Wednesday that “it feels like a scary movie.”

The family, she said, “have to go back through the experience — once again. Once again, because you don’t know where he’s at, but you know what he’s capable of.”

Also Wednesday, the reward for information leading to Morley’s arrest increased from $2,500 to $15,000, a combined sum from the Tennessee governor’s office, the Tennessee Bureau of Investigation and the U.S. Marshals Service, the bureau said.

Fitts said he wished Morley had been caught, but he urged people not to take things into their own hands.

“This is really frustrating that we can’t find him. Somebody will eventually, I have no doubt in my mind. I wish it was us so we could catch him now,” Fitts said. “But, and I just want to let our people know that, again, don’t approach him. Call 911. We’ll handle it.”

Around 11:30 p.m. Sunday, prison staff members confirmed that Morley was unaccounted for and conducted an emergency count and search of the facility, the Tennessee Department of Correction said.

Security video showed Morley “breached the perimeter fence and exited the facility in his TDOC uniform,” the department said.

The department has not given more details about how the escape occurred, which it said Wednesday is under investigation.

#70. RFK Jr. to ease access to unapproved peptides, draft policy states

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

October 28, 2026 / 9:20 PM EDT / CBS News

Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. took steps this week that could open up access to certain unapproved peptide drugs that are being promoted for wellness.

In a draft policy filed Wednesday, Kennedy said he had determined that compounding pharmacies could prepare six specific peptides — BPC-157, Epitalon, KPV, MOTS-c, Semax, TB-500 — even though they do not have approval from the Food and Drug Administration.

"By allowing limited compounding of the substances addressed in this guidance under appropriate safeguards, the policy provides patients with a more controlled alternative," Kennedy wrote.

Compounding pharmacies mix ingredients to prepare customized medications for individual patients.

Although they are not FDA approved, the six peptides in Kennedy's new policy are often still marketed with health claims.

The Department of Health and Human Services "did not intend to take action" against licensed pharmacies or physicians that compound "a drug product using a peptide bulk drug substance that is not a component of an FDA-approved drug product," Kennedy's policy said.

Peptides are small proteins that can have powerful effects. There are several, such as insulin, that are FDA approved as being safe and effective. GLP-1 medications used for weight loss and to treat diabetes also contain FDA approved peptides.

In July, an FDA advisory panel voted to recommend allowing the six unapproved peptides to be used in compounding, although that decision did not constitute FDA approval. That vote, however, went against the recommendations of the FDA's own scientists, who said there isn't enough evidence of safety and effectiveness for each of the six peptides to make such a determination.

Kennedy cited that panel's recommendation in his decision, saying he had determined the policy "advances patient choice and freedom while appropriately protecting patient safety."

In a statement provided to CBS News, an HHS spokesperson said that pharmacies will have to do quality testing and safety reporting, and that should help patients know what is in these peptide drugs that are currently being sold by unregulated sources.

"The policy does not grant FDA approval or establish clinical safety and effectiveness," the HHS spokesperson said. "It requires patient-specific prescriptions, traceable ingredients, quality testing, and safety reporting to hold compounders accountable and give patients an alternative to unregulated sellers."

The draft policy requires an FDA-registered ingredient maker, a lab certificate for the raw ingredients, and reports of serious side effects.

In a CBS News investigation published last week, a laboratory test was conducted on an experimental peptide weight-loss drug known as retatrutide that was purchased from a bodega in Brooklyn, New York.

The lab test, however, found that the sample of retatrutide purchased from the bodega did not contain a peptide at all.

A CBS News investigation last summer found that dozens of clinics across the country, staffed by licensed physicians and nurse practitioners, have been openly advertising retatrutide even though it has not been reviewed by the FDA and remains in clinical trials by drug giant Eli Lilly.

Kennedy's draft policy will be open to public comment beginning Friday through Nov. 13. The policy will remain in place until the FDA finalizes a rule regarding compounded peptides. However, at this point, the FDA has not proposed one.

#94. CEO says plan is to file for bankruptcy after ending luxury never-ending cruise

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

October 28, 2026 / 8:31 PM EDT / CBS News

More than 200 passengers, mostly Americans, are stranded in Cambodia after the operator of a luxury U.S. cruise ship that was on a continuous around-the-world voyage ran out of money due to skyrocketing fuel costs.

Villa Vie Residences, the Florida-based operator of the MV Odyssey, said the war with Iran quadrupled fuel costs and forced it to suspend operations on Tuesday.

CEO Mikael Petterson told CBS News in a phone interview Wednesday that Villa Vie Residences was paying an average of $700 a ton for fuel before the war broke out in late February. Since then, it has been paying anywhere from $2,000 to $3,000 per ton.

The Odyssey uses between 700 and 800 tons per month in fuel, Petterson disclosed.

"When you divide it over 300 people on board, it's just backbreaking," Petterson told CBS News. "And this has been going on for nine months. We've tried to do everything we can do."

In a statement, Villa Vie Residences said the per cabin fuel costs rose from an average of $1,800 per month before the war to between $6,000 and $8,000.

The company was unwilling to pass those rising costs on to its passengers, Petterson said.

The Odyssey launched in October 2024 for a continuous around-the-world journey. At the time, passengers could become residents of the ship by purchasing a permanent cabin for several hundred thousand dollars. Some sold their homes to do so. At the time, they were given a 15-year minimum guarantee of residency aboard the Odyssey.

The Odyssey attempted to counter the spiking fuel prices by slowing down its itinerary and spending five or six days in each port "hoping that things would come to a close in the Middle East," Petterson said, but it wasn't enough.

"For us, when our monthly expenses go up from $400,000 to $2 million, where are we going to get the $1.6 million?" Petterson asked.

The Villa Vie Residences CEO said that the ship ran out of money as it searched for a financial partner, and the plan is now to file for Chapter 11 bankruptcy.

"We've borrowed money, we've stretched our payrolls as far as we can, and we're at a point where it's just at a breaking point where we need capitalization," Petterson said.

Passenger Benjamin Schatz thought the MV Odyssey would be his forever home.

"Honestly, right now, I'm in denial and in shock, and I'm heartbroken," Schatz, a retiree, told CBS News.

CBS News met Schatz in April 2025 for a story featuring Villa Vie Residences. At that point, he had been living on the ship for about one year. On Wednesday, he was given just hours to pack up his life and leave.

Said Schatz: "There are people now who are homeless and who have very little money and don't know what they're going to do."

Alaska Democratic Senate candidate Mary Peltola's campaign is under fire after a New York Times report Wednesday that it played a role in recruiting Dan J. Sullivan -- a Republican who almost has the same name as incumbent GOP Sen. Dan Sullivan -- as part of an effort to sow confusion and ultimately benefit Peltola's campaign.

The Times report said it relied on 12 sources, text messages and documents that allegedly showed members of Peltola's campaign, lawyers for Peltola and senior aides at the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee contributed to pushing Dan J. Sullivan, a Republican on Alaska's four-way ballot, to run.

The Times said the effort to find a spoiler candidate -- who could potentially siphon votes from Sen. Sullivan and benefit Peltola -- was internally referred to as the ballot composition project.

Alaskans for Mary, the campaign committee for Peltola, said through a spokesperson on Wednesday that it "has no involvement with either Sullivan campaign."

The DSCC told ABC News in a statement that it was "not involved with Dan J. Sullivan."

"Republicans have a long history of recruiting and propping up candidates in an attempt to boost their own campaigns," a DSCC spokesperson wrote in the statement, detailing an instance when Peltola ran for reelection in the House in 2024 and the state Republican Party supported an effort by a Democratic House candidate and inmate in New York federal prison to stay on the ballot, challenging Peltola.

Peltola finished first in Alaska's August primary with 49.5% of the vote and Sen. Sullivan was second with 41.4%. Dan J. Sullivan was third with 2.5% of the vote and Democrat David Leslie finished with 1.1%. Leslie later dropped out of the race and Republican Gerald Heikes, who finished fifth in the primary with 1%, moved into the fourth spot on the November ballot.

On the general election ballot, Sen. Sullivan is listed as Dan S. Sullivan, a registered Republican and it is noted that he is the incumbent. The other Sullivan is listed as Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. with no party affiliation.

In response to the Times report, President Donald Trump said in a social media post Wednesday afternoon that Peltola should "suffer the Legal and Political consequences for what she has done," and that she and Dan J. Sullivan should be kicked off the ballot. The president also said Peltola engaged in "election fraud."

"Dan Sullivan should be allowed to run without either Mary Peltola or FAKE Dan Sullivan on the Ballot," Trump wrote.

ABC News has reached out to Dan J. Sullivan's campaign for comment on the New York Times' report. ABC News also reached out to the Alaska Department of Elections for comment.

Alaska courts evaluated the dispute over Dan J. Sullivan's candidacy and found nothing illegal about his bid to be on the ballot -- even if it was not entirely in "good faith" or an attempt to "confuse and mislead" as the Trump administration and Republicans contend.

Ballot-stacking tactics are common in politics and are widely considered legal, the New York Times reported.

Former Peltola staffer Elisa Rios told the Times about the reported recruitment effort, saying Peltola wasn't directly involved but that she was "definitely aware."

However, the Times also reported that Rios later requested a retraction of the statements she gave the newspaper, though she did not disclose a reason.

#105. Experts react to new findings on CTE in former NFL players

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Updated on: October 28, 2026 / 8:27 PM EDT / CBS News

A new study from Boston University estimating that nearly 75% of NFL players who died over a 15-year period had signs of CTE in their brains is raising concern, and experts say it demonstrates the need to collect more data on the condition.

CTE, or chronic traumatic encephalopathy, is a degenerative brain disease found in people who have suffered repeated blows to the head. It can only be definitively diagnosed after death. It has also been found in the brains of other athletes, first responders and military members, but is rarely seen in the general population.

The Boston University research builds on data published in The British Medical Journal in August. That data estimated that anywhere from 25% to 98% of ex-NFL players who died between 2016 and 2021 had CTE. The broad estimate reflected uncertainty about how representative brain donors studied were of the overall NFL population, since brain donors are more likely to have symptoms believed to be related to CTE.

The Boston researchers looked for signs of the condition in brains donated by 387 players who died between 2008 and 2023 — a subset of the 2,362 former NFL players who died during that period. Ninety-four percent of the donors had some form of CTE. The researchers estimated that former NFL players with cognitive symptoms were 5 times more likely to have their brains donated to science than others, and estimated from there that 74.5% of players had some form of CTE. The research is published in JAMA Network Open.

CBS News chief medical correspondent Dr. Jon LaPook cautioned that that figure may be an overestimate. He noted that the research looked at how many former players had signs of CTE in their brain — not how many showed actual symptoms of CTE.

Dr. Kristen Dams-O'Connor, director of the Brain Injury Research Center at Mount Sinai, said the results of the paper "cannot and should not be interpreted as being representative of current living athletes." She pointed out that of those whose brains were studied, "many ... ended their careers long ago." In recent years, the NFL has implemented rule changes that are designed to reduce concussions on the field, and introduced more protective equipment, including Guardian Caps.

"This should translate to lower CTE risk in recent generations of players, compared to those who played decades ago," Dams-O'Connor said.

The NFL told CBS News in a statement that it "continuously strives to make the game of football safer, including by implementing strategies to reduce concussions and head impacts."

Dams-O'Connor said that while Boston University used "strong methods" to adjust for selection bias in the data, there are still other gaps, such as accounting for the approximately 20,000 NFL players who could have died during the study's 15-year timeframe but did not. She also said the results could not be applied to the "vast majority of people who played youth football" or other non-professional contact sports.

"It's a helpful study in trying to look at the bigger picture, recognizing that those who donate their brain are more likely to have cognitive symptoms, and often are more likely to have had changes," said Dr. Jeremy Tanner, assistant professor of neurology at The Glenn Biggs Institute for Alzheimer's and Neurological Diseases at the UT San Antonio Health Science Center. "Even taking that selection bias into account, when you look more broadly, it's at a minimum 20 to 25%, which is eye-opening and really shows the need to both advance the research."

Dr. Jay Alberts, director of the Cleveland Clinic Concussion Center, said the data shows the need to look for early warning signs of CTE.

"We often think about neurological disease, including CTE, as having a silent phase. These things are changing, and we can't detect them," Alberts said. "But I don't think it's silent, per se. I think what we need to do is listen more carefully and use better measurements to understand what those changes are."

Those measurements could include studying a person's "instrumental activities of daily living," or IADLs, Alberts said. That includes skills like driving a car or balancing a checkbook, where a person must use both cognitive function and motor function simultaneously. In patients with Alzheimer's or Parkinson's disease, IADLs start to change between five and seven years before a diagnosis is made. He said CTE may be similar.

"Those types of activities are really insights into brain function, and I would argue those are things that would help us to listen more effectively," he said.

Understanding how a person's brain function may change during the early stages of CTE could make it easier to diagnose, Alberts said. That could also lead to interventions that might "slow it or even potentially reverse it."

Alberts and Tanner also highlighted research focused on determining if there are biomarker changes that could be connected to CTE. Ongoing studies, including one that has a study site at UT Health San Antonio, follow current or former players throughout their life to see if there are any abnormal changes to the body or brain over the years. Alberts called for even more long-term research, and Tanner said that such studies can benefit more than just former NFL players.

"This has really been a rapid area of scientific advancement over the last 10 to 20 years, and has led to breakthroughs in the new therapies for Alzheimer's disease, and hopefully (will) for many other neurodegenerative diseases like CTE, Lewy body disease, and others in the years to come," Tanner said.

"Hopefully we can develop tools and biomarkers to detect these brain changes during life, which will enable us to study these diseases during life, and in the best case, learn ways to better prevent or treat these diseases."

#115. High-speed train crash in Spain kills at least 68, dozens injured

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Updated on: October 28, 2026 / 8:26 PM EDT / CBS/AP

A high-speed train slammed into the rear of another train that had stopped in central Spain, derailing two cars and killing at least 68 people Wednesday morning, according to local authorities.

The regional government of Castilla La Mancha confirmed the death toll and said another 58 people were receiving medical treatment at hospitals. Rescue workers continue searching the wreckage for survivors and bodies.

The crash occurred Wednesday morning in the province of Toledo, according to Spain's rail system operator ADIF. Rail service was suspended between Madrid, Ciudad Real and Andalusia.

Padilla told state broadcaster TVE the accident was between two high-speed trains heading in the same direction from Madrid to Seville and that one ran into the tail of the other while it was stopped, knocking two of its cars off the track.

The accident involved a train from Spain's state-owned RENFE company and the private rail company IRYO. Trains from those two companies were also involved in a deadly crash in January. Padilla said 397 passengers were in the RENFE train and 147 passengers were in the IRYO train.

"Today all of Spain is grieving," Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez said from the crash site, where he announced that his government would declare three days of national mourning.

Sánchez called for increased safety measures to restore confidence in a high-speed rail system that was once a source of national pride.

"It is unacceptable for a country like Spain to suffer two high-speed rail accidents in the same year," said Sánchez, who faces a national election on Nov. 29.

Spain's Transport Minister Óscar Puente said the crash was caused by what he called the "inexplicable" order from a central command center for Spain's trains after a security system had failed. He said the train engineers were not to blame.

Puente said that at 8:30 a.m. the security system on a stretch of 63 kilometers (39 miles) stopped working due to a computer malfunction, causing all trains on that stretch to stop. But the central command told the RENFE train to start running again without any speed restrictions, and it ended up colliding with the stopped IRYO train at 8:53 a.m.

Puente said the train should never have been told to start moving again since there was still a "stop signal" active on that line. And if it had, at least it should have been told to go at reduced speed.

Authorities were still studying information to see how fast the train was going, but it could have been at 200 kph (124 mph) for that stretch of the track, Puente said.

"I am not attributing responsibility to anyone, I am just stating the facts," Puente said.

The crash, which occurred in a flat stretch surrounded by olive fields in between the towns of Urda and Los Yébenes, left carriages shredded into bits of metal and other material, scattering debris down a gravel slope.

Spanish Health Minister Mónica García said that emergency workers were focused on rescuing survivors and bodies from the most damaged train cars.

"It was a high-speed impact, so we are expecting this to be a tragedy," García said.

One passenger said, "We were all thrown into the air." He said he was "reasonably OK" but that his friend was in much worse condition.

Passenger Ignacio Relaño was traveling on the RENFE train. He told state broadcaster TVE that the train had stopped momentarily on the line before it started again and then crashed into the IRYO.

"We all felt the train abruptly slowing down and then the impact," he said. "The car leaned to one side like it was about to tip over. I hit my nose, everything went flying, luggage, cell phones. Everything shook."

Another passenger recounted to Cadena Ser radio how she felt the train slam on the brakes and was thrown forward in her seat, breaking the small table in front of her. She said she saw body parts and trapped people covered in blood.

The mayor of the nearby town of Urda, Manuel Galán, told Spanish state broadcaster TVE that civil protection authorities had asked his town hall to prepare a sports pavilion to attend to passengers.

In January, 45 people died in southern Spain when a train jumped the track and hit an oncoming train. The crash was the deadliest in Spain since a 2013 crash that killed 80 people. The January incident shook the confidence the European country has in its extensive high-speed rail network.
