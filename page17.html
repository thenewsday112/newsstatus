#209. A Ukrainian war robot used AI to complete a 12-mile supply run almost entirely on its own. The unit says it's a combat first.

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A Ukrainian military unit said on Wednesday that it successfully sent a ground robot on a 12-mile supply run, using AI to navigate for nearly the entirety of the mission.

The Lava Unmanned Systems Regiment of the 2nd Corps, or Khartiia Corps, of Ukraine's National Guard announced the completion of the autonomous logistics mission in a statement shared with Business Insider, calling it the "first of its kind under combat conditions."

The unit said that the robot completed 93% of its 20-kilometer trip on its own, delivering cargo and returning to its starting point autonomously, with an operator intervening for only 1.4 kilometers of the journey.

Before departure, a separate ground robot equipped with light detection and ranging technology, or LiDAR, mapped the route and terrain in 3D. Cameras on the supply bot detected obstacles while AI-powered navigation guided the robot.

"This mission has shown that autonomous ground systems are already capable of operating in real combat conditions," said Andrii Kopach, who serves as commander of the Lava Regiment's Unmanned Ground Systems Battalion.

"The biggest change is that the operator is no longer a driver but a dispatcher," he said. "Instead of controlling the vehicle every second, they monitor its movement and step in only when needed."

Kopach said that the unit's next ambitions are to enable a single operator to oversee multiple robots at once and expand autonomous missions to casualty evacuations. He added that more AI-powered ground robots would reduce dependence on "a constant control link," such as Starlink, a satellite internet service which recently saw major speed disruptions in Ukraine, negatively affecting front-line operations.

Ground robots have become an important tool for Ukraine, carrying out resupply, casualty evacuation, and assault missions. The Ukrainian military, and Western military leaders studying lessons from the war, hope that increased reliance on ground robots and aerial drones, as well as the increased integration of AI capabilities into these systems, can help reduce the risks to the troops who would otherwise perform those missions on increasingly dangerous battlefields.

Microsoft just posted the first quarter of its 2027 fiscal financial results. The software maker made $92.3 billion in revenue and a net income of $33.6 billion during Q1. Revenue is up 19 percent, and net income has increased by 32 percent.

It’s a new financial year for Microsoft, so the company is changing up how it reports its finances by splitting its businesses into two segments for the first time. Gone are the previous segments: Productivity and Business Processes, Intelligent Cloud, and More Personal Computing. It’s now just Agents and Infra, and Devices and Consumer.

Agents and Infra, which includes businesses like Azure, Microsoft 365 commercial and consumer, and server products, makes up 83 percent ($76.8 billion) of the overall $92.3 billion of Microsoft’s revenue and has increased 25 percent year-over-year. The big changes to Microsoft’s financial reporting also mean we will now see the company disclose Azure revenue on a quarterly basis. Azure contributed $33.17 billion in revenue this quarter, up 48 percent year-over-year.

Devices and Consumer, which includes Windows, Xbox, Surface, and Microsoft’s search and advertising businesses, makes up the remaining 17 percent ($15.5 billion) of Microsoft’s $92.3 billion in revenues this quarter — a decrease of three percent.

Windows OEM and devices revenue is down 22 percent year-over-year, amid a RAMageddon crisis that has seen the overall PC market dropped by 20 percent in the recent quarter. Microsoft blames the revenue drop on the benefit of Windows 10 end of support during the prior year, as well as OEM inventory drawdowns and a weaker PC market.

Microsoft launched Intel- and Qualcomm-powered Surface Pro 12 and Surface Laptop 8 devices just before its new financial year began, and the company even refreshed its smaller Surface Pro and Surface Laptop with Qualcomm’s X2 Plus chips during the quarter. Surface prices are higher than ever thanks to RAMageddon, and the PC market looks like it’s going to go through a very tough time during Microsoft’s 2027 financial year and beyond.

Microsoft CFO Amy Hood said on the company’s earnings call that she expects Windows OEM revenue to decline again next quarter, around “the mid 30s,” and a decline is expected to continue throughout the 2027 financial year.Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella also remarked on the company’s new hybrid AI push for Windows during the earnings call. “We are executing on our vision to bring hybrid intelligence to every desk in every home, making every PC a secure agent box,” said Nadella. “As more capable models run locally, we see an opportunity to make more intelligence effectively unmetered.”

Microsoft and Nvidia unveiled a range of RTX Spark laptops earlier this month, including the Surface Laptop Ultra. The laptops ship with up to 128GB of unified memory, and are capable of running recent frontier models locally.

The first quarter of the 2027 financial year for Xbox has been dominated by a “reset” with thousands of layoffs and studios spun off. The layoffs aren’t over yet, with rumors more could arrive in November and January. In a memo earlier this year, Xbox CEO Asha Sharma said “we will return Xbox to player and revenue growth” by the end of fiscal year 2027 (which runs through next June).

The aim, as Sharma puts it, is to “scale what works” so that by fiscal year 2030 Xbox is “halfway to our long-term daily-player goal with sustained double-digit growth in players and engagement and industry leading margins.” Sharma posted on X that Xbox was “making progress, with a lot more work ahead.” Sharma also revealed that PC platform revenue has grown in double digits this quarter, and that “console sales remain constrained by supply, with more demand than consoles available.”

Hood says Xbox hardware revenue will decline again next quarter, but Xbox content and services revenue is expected to increase in the “mid to high single digits.” Sharma says Microsoft is “working to get more consoles into players’ hands, including through our expanded refurbishment program.”

Microsoft’s cloud revenues were really the star of the earnings show again this quarter, a phrase I seem to always write every few months. Microsoft Cloud revenue contributed $63.9 billion towards Microsoft’s revenues this quarter, almost 70 percent. That’s up 30 percent year-over-year, thanks to strong demand for Azure and Microsoft 365 commercial cloud.

Nadella also discussed his vision for Microsoft’s own AI models on the company’s earnings call. “We also continue to make rapid progress with our first-party models,” said Nadella. “Our goal is to advance the cost to outcome frontier, which means delivering capabilities comparable to leading models within three to six months of their release at a fraction of the training cost, thereby allowing us to transfer those cost benefits to customers.”

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Federal Trade Commission is asking companies from food delivery services to airlines about how they use personalized prices.

The Commission said on Wednesday that it sent orders to the companies asking for details about what kind of personal information they use to determine prices. The orders target a wide range of companies, from retailers Walmart and Target to food delivery services Uber and DoorDash to airlines like Delta and United.

"Consumers deserve to know whether companies use their web surfing habits or other personal data to estimate the highest price they can charge each consumer," said FTC Chairman Andrew N. Ferguson.

"Workers also deserve to know if their compensation is affected by how companies use their personal data," Ferguson added.

Several companies pushed back against the FTC's claims.

"There is no fare product Delta has ever used, is testing or plans to use that targets customers with individualized offers based on personal data," a Delta spokesperson said.

"We do not use personal information to set prices, and we never will," Instacart said in a statement.

Walmart and Target previously said they would be transparent about their pricing strategies as more stores start using digital price tags.

A growing number of companies are using dynamic pricing to determine what customers pay for everything from concert tickets to jeans.

#228. Dissents, rising prices, and a change to the meeting schedule: 3 takeaways from the October Fed meeting

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The second-to-last Federal Open Market Committee meeting in 2026 has come and gone, with some new information unveiled at Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference.

The FOMC decided in its meeting to hold interest rates steady on Wednesday, although two members dissented.

That comes as the inflation rate has been above the Fed's 2% target, while unemployment remains low. Warsh also announced a shake-up to next year's FOMC meeting plans.

Here are three large takeaways from Wednesday's Fed meeting.

While last month's 25-basis-point hike was a unanimous 12-0 decision, the October vote to hold rates steady was 10-2. Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack wanted to hike rates by 25 basis points again.

"The skew is toward tightening, and today's decision reads as a disagreement over timing rather than over destination," Jason Pride, chief of investment strategy and research at Glenmede, said in commentary.

Warsh detailed some positives about the employment side of the dual mandate, including low four-week moving average unemployment claims, a lower jobless rate than a year prior, and better job growth than last year.

Meanwhile, the other side of the dual mandate, price stability, is more of a concern. Inflation has remained above the Fed's 2% target. The Fed hiked rates in September to try to help that. Warsh said at Wednesday's press conference that the FOMC's discussion has been largely focused on price stability.

"Energy and related prices have moved higher again in recent weeks," he said. "And our job is to ensure that relative price changes in some sectors do not broaden out across the economy and that inflation expectations remain as they are anchored."

Personally identifiable information belonging to thousands of U.S. Department of Homeland Security employees has been published online after a hacker stole reams of data in a previously undisclosed breach at the government department earlier this year.

The breach includes names, job titles, and work contact information, such as email addresses, cell phone numbers, and office locations, of more than 40,000 people who work for the federal department, including border and immigration agents, cybersecurity staffers, and senior department officials.

A hacker who goes by the handle Department of Peace claimed they obtained the data from an Okta single sign-on system used by Homeland Security to allow employees and contractors across its various agencies to log in to its central network. The Okta system also contains IT and log-in records associated with the employees’ user account, including details about which systems and internal apps they are permitted to access.

More than half of the dataset — some 21,000 records — relate to employees with U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP), who work at airports and other ports of entry.

The data also contains records associated with another 10,000 employees of Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) and its Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) unit, and just under a 1,000 employees who work at U.S. cybersecurity defense agency CISA.

Thousands of additional records relate to staff who work at the department’s headquarters and its other agencies, including U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services and the Federal Protective Service.

“They have our information, so it’s only fair that we have theirs,” said Department of Peace in a note left with the data, which TechCrunch has seen. The hacker did not respond to TechCrunch’s email requesting comment and with questions about how the breach was carried out.

This is the latest data breach to hit the U.S. government in recent months, following earlier hacks involving the theft of thousands of FBI agents’ personnel records and the personal information of millions of serving and former military service personnel from the Pentagon’s human resources unit. It’s also the second breach involving Homeland Security this year, after the same hacker claimed to have taken thousands of contracts between the department and the private sector, including defense contractors and surveillance vendors.

The latest batch of breached data, which appears authentic, offers a rare — albeit incomplete — view into the scale and operations of Homeland Security, whose deportations, arbitrary detentions, and killings of U.S. citizens under its leadership have become a key issue for voters ahead of the upcoming U.S. midterm elections.

The stolen Homeland Security data was provided to journalist and hacker maia arson crimew, who then shared a copy with DDoSecrets, a nonprofit collective that hosts leaked datasets in the public interest.

TechCrunch verified a portion of the data as authentic by matching the names of several known Homeland Security employees against an offline copy of the database. The thousands of employee directory records appear to have been exfiltrated around mid-August, according to the timestamps on each person’s record. The number of leaked records is equivalent to about one-fifth of Homeland Security’s workforce, which totals around 220,000 staff.

It’s unclear whether the vulnerability that allowed the breach has been fixed or if Homeland Security is aware of the data theft.

A spokesperson for Homeland Security told TechCrunch after publication: “DHS is aware of a potential breach and is actively working to address the situation swiftly and effectively. We take all security threats against our employees and officers seriously and will work to ensure all are properly protected. We will update the public when more information is available.”

When reached, Okta said it was aware of this report. “Okta is secure, and we have no evidence our data or systems were compromised,” a spokesperson said.

While the leaked data includes identifiable information about agents and officers, the dataset also reveals more about the surveillance technologies and cybersecurity tools that Homeland Security employees may be able to access. Critics have called for investigations into tech companies that fund or provide their technology to ICE.

The leaked data contains information about employees’ membership in user groups within the department’s Active Directory, a system that controls access to the department’s internal tools, services, and third-party offerings used for work.

Most of the Homeland Security employees in the dataset were members of the user group that had access to Microsoft 365, the tech giant’s cloud service that hosts the agency’s emails and files, as well as user groups for accessing Adobe Acrobat, Citrix, and other typical desktop software.

The data also shows that around 4,500 users, including ICE agents and deportation officers, are members of a group called “Headspace_users.” Online therapy provider Headspace previously had an $82,500 federal contract with ICE that ended in 2023 to fund “mental health software required for ERO employees,” a reference to ICE’s deportation unit.

A spokesperson for Headspace said the company has not provided any services to DHS or ICE since 2023.

Dozens of other user groups reference Penlink, a web surveillance company that makes technology it claims can track the location data of hundreds of millions of mobile devices. Penlink has had a contract with ICE for years. The user groups vary in size, from a handful of users in local Homeland Security offices around the country to more than 2,000 users across ICE’s HSI unit nationally. One group specifically references Penlink PLX, which the company says is used to capture and analyze “real-time intelligence” from phone calls, texts, web activity, and social media.

A spokesperson for Penlink did not respond to a request for comment.

One user group also includes more than 200 CBP users associated with Mobile Fortify, a facial-recognition app used by both CBP and ICE agents to identify people and verify their information and immigration status.

The user groups also include several employees associated with ChatGPT access, including senior DHS officials, as well as other groups associated with investigations into the China-backed hacking groups known as Salt Typhoon and Volt Typhoon.

Researchers and public information advocates say that the exposed data could provide valuable leads for investigations into alleged abuses by the Trump administration, such as identifying email addresses of agents that can be used in public records requests under the Freedom of Information Act.

“Email addresses and phone numbers are indispensable tools for FOIA researchers, often making the difference between a vague records request that gets stonewalled and one that gets results,” said Ryan Shapiro, the executive director of transparency nonprofit Property of the People.

“Given the Trump administration’s contempt for even basic transparency, the need for such tools is urgent,” said Shapiro.

DDoSecrets co-founder and journalist Emma Best said that the exposed Homeland Security data will be a “catalyst for investigations, empowering journalists, Congressional inquiries, legal challenges, community action and Freedom of Information Act requests.”

#216. Bessent says 'not necessarily' a mistake if the Fed hikes again

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Wednesday that further Federal Reserve rate hikes wouldn't necessarily be a mistake.

After CNBC's Brian Sullivan asked if rate increases in the future would constitute a monetary policy error, Bessent responded: "That's not necessarily so."

Bessent's comments came hours after the Fed announced it would keep interest rates unchanged at its October gathering, as the market widely expected.

Bessent is a key confidant of President Donald Trump, who has repeatedly called for lower interest rates. Trump pressured Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh's predeccesor, Jerome Powell, over the issue during Powell's final months at the helm of the central bank.

But Bessent said Wednesday that both he and Trump believe Warsh is doing well and can strike a balance between ensuring full employment and stable prices.

"I think Chair Warsh is off to a great start," Bessent said. "As I often say, I have great confidence in him."

The Fed's decision to hold rates steady came a month after it lifted borrowing costs for the first time in three years. Fed funds futures traders widely expected the central bank to return to rate hiking when it meets again in December.

Bessent said he would like to see the Fed have an "open mind" about the supply shock from energy prices having a "transitory nature." The U.S. war with Iran has led to a sharp drop in global crude production volumes, in turn leading annual inflation readings to move further from the Fed's target of 2%.

Bessent also said the U.S. efforts to isolate the Iran economy, dubbed Operation Economic Outcast, have been "very successful." He said internal estimates show that Iran's economy could contract by as much as 25% in the span of a year.

When asked about bond yields on artificial-intelligence related debt, the Wall Street veteran said "we monitor" it, but that it didn't need to cause concern.

Drinking plenty of water is essential to staying hydrated, but sometimes, H20 alone doesn’t cut it, says Dr. Matthew Sedgley, a physician specializing in sports medicine at MedStar Health. Mixing electrolyte powder into your water is a fast and convenient way to do that, says Amy Goldsmith, a registered dietitian specializing in sports nutrition who owns Kindred Nutrition & Kinetics. Before buying electrolyte powder, it’s helpful to understand what it is, what benefits it offers and who it’s designed for. Here’s some key information experts want you to know.

Electrolytes are minerals like sodium, potassium and calcium that help fluids enter and exit your cells to keep you hydrated, says Jay Patruno, a registered dietitian and certified personal trainer. (They’re called electrolytes because they have an electrical charge when dissolved in water or bodily fluids.) These minerals also play an important role in muscle and nerve contraction, creating energy and building tissue, says Michael Reavis Jr., a clinical dietitian at the University of Maryland Medical Center.

Your body doesn’t make electrolytes: You consume them through food. All foods have electrolytes, but some are richer in specific minerals than others, says Patruno. For example, salt is rich in sodium and chloride, bananas are rich in potassium, yogurt is rich in calcium and nuts are rich in magnesium, says Reavis. You mainly lose electrolytes through sweat, though your kidneys also filter out excess electrolytes in urine, says Lisa Young, a registered nutritionist and an adjunct professor of nutrition at New York University.

Everyone loses electrolytes at different volumes depending on overall health status, level of physical activity and sweat rate, meaning how many ounces of fluid you lose per hour while sweating, says Goldsmith. Your doctor can evaluate these things to determine how you should go about replacing electrolytes.

Electrolyte powder is a mixture of minerals like sodium, potassium and magnesium that you add to water to make a fortified drink. It’s a type of electrolyte product, like chews, drops and gels. Since you dissolve electrolyte powder in water, they help balance your body’s fluid and electrolyte levels simultaneously, which is key to staying hydrated, says Young.

Electrolyte powders are designed to replace electrolytes you lose, mainly through sweat, and support fast rehydration so you avoid symptoms of depletion, like fatigue, blood pressure irregularities and lightheadedness, says Kelly Jones, a sports dietitian who works with professional and student-athletes. “They help you get a significant amount of electrolytes in a very short amount of time, give you all the different types of minerals you need at once, and you know exactly how much of each one you’re consuming,” says Goldsmith. “There’s not really a food or drink that can naturally do that.”

It’s important to note that while electrolyte products like powders can help you stay hydrated, they’re not a replacement for plain water, says Sedgley. Electrolyte products should be used as needed in specific situations like intense exercise, while water is essential to drink throughout the day to stay hydrated and keep up your fluid levels, he says.

The most important electrolytes to replace are sodium, which you lose at the highest rate while sweating, and potassium, which comes in second, says Jones. Electrolytes like calcium, magnesium, chloride and phosphorus are also important for hydration, but if you exercise for less than two hours at a time, the amount you lose is negligible, says Jones. So focus on getting these nutrients through food, she says.

Electrolyte powders mainly benefit the average person who engages in an hour or more of moderate to intense exercise, says Sedgley. Moderate exercise involves activities like brisk walking or light jogging, where talking is a bit more challenging than usual, and intense exercise involves activities like fast running, cycling or high-intensity interval training, where talking is difficult, he says.

Electrolyte powders also support recovery and hydration for people who do physical activity in the heat, says Sedgley. That includes runners, bikers, those playing in sporting events, construction workers and landscapers, for example. But in hot conditions, even low-intensity exercise like walking may leave you feeling depleted, he says. People who spend all day on their feet, like teachers and medical professionals, can also benefit from electrolyte powders, says Jones.
